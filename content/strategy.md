2026-11-23 · @rclf
## How this doc is used
From EP04 (Tue 24 Nov, 8 PM) every reel is picked fresh: the build run reads this doc, scans the last 72 hours of cyber-crime news, and chooses the day's topic inside the weekly rhythm below.
- **Who reads it:** the two daily build runs (9:40 AM builds the 8 PM reel; 6:40 PM builds the next day's 7 AM reel). Where this doc and the repo's CLAUDE.md differ on topics, audience or format, this doc wins. The legal rules in CLAUDE.md always win over everything.
- **To change direction:** edit any section here. The next run follows the new version.
- **To add an idea:** add a line to the Ideas inbox at the end. The next run whose slot fits picks it up and ticks it.
- **Unchanged:** two reels a day (7 AM and 8 PM), Hindi voice, English on-screen text, 35–45 seconds (shortened from 90–120 on 25 Nov 2026 to lift watch time), review and approval by email.

## The five-part spine of every reel
Every reel touches all five parts, so every reel carries the scenario, the technology, the forensics and the law. The series decides which part gets the most time.
| # | Part | Time | On screen | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The scenario (cold open) | 0–8 s | Frame 1 is the hook: the stake in big text (a ₹ amount or what happens to the viewer) and the first spoken line both start at 0 s, with no logo, title, series or chapter card before them; then the dramatised mock phone (call, WhatsApp, SMS or UPI screen) with a DRAMATISED tag | Stop the scroll; the viewer sees themselves |
| 2 | How it works | 8–18 s | Animated diagram of the technology (APK overlay, UPI rails, SIM, WhatsApp Web session) | The technical part — explain, never enable |
| 3 | The trail | 18–26 s | Money graph, CDR or IPDR table, device extraction, hash values | How criminals get caught (forensics) |
| 4 | The law | 26–33 s | One law card: the key section in plain words (BNS, BNSS, IT Act, BSA or DPDP), plus at most one sourced number card; every other section goes in the caption | The advocate's authority |
| 5 | Do this now | 33–42 s | A "screenshot this" card: 3 red flags and 3 actions (1930, cybercrime.gov.in, Chakshu, the bank), then a send line, spoken and on screen, naming who needs it ("Send this to someone who takes UPI payments") | Drives saves and shares |

Then a 3-second outro (logo, "Legal awareness only · Not legal advice", laws cited).
Weighting by series: Tech Decoded spends longest on part 2, Forensics File and Real Case Breakdown on part 3, Know Your Rights on part 4, Scam Alert and Follow the Money on parts 1 and 5, Corporate Cyber Brief on parts 1, 4 and 5. In the 35–45 second format a part can be a single beat; the series' weighted part gets the spare seconds.
## Daily trend scan (the step that keeps it fresh)
Before scripting, every build run scans the last 72 hours of news and writes what it found to `E/trends.md`.
1. **Search** the last 72 hours, with at least one extended search:
   - Official: I4C / MHA, PIB Fact Check, CERT-In, RBI, NPCI, SEBI, DoT / Sanchar Saathi advisories.
   - Police: state and city cyber police press notes (Hyderabad, Cyberabad, Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Gujarat, Kerala and others).
   - News: The Hindu, Indian Express, Times of India, Economic Times, Business Standard, Business Today, Moneylife, The420.
   - Legal: LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, SCC Online (judgments, new rules, rules coming into force).
2. **List 5 candidate stories** and score each 1–5 on: fresh (new in 72 hours), reach (how many Indians it can hit), fit (to the slot's series and audience), verifiable (a primary source exists), safe (no victim identity, no prejudging a pending case, no how-to). Maximum 25.
3. **Decide:**
   - 8 PM Scam Alert, Follow the Money and This Week slots: a story scoring 20 or more becomes the topic; the planned topic goes back to the topic bank.
   - Other slots: keep the planned topic, but use a fresh connected story as the hook or the scenario.
   - Big breaking news (a new I4C, CERT-In or RBI advisory, a Supreme Court order, a rule coming into force, a scam wave with large reported losses) can take any slot that has not yet been sent for review.
4. **No repeats:** check the last 14 days of `content/calendar.md`. The same topic comes back within 14 days only with a genuinely new development, and the hook then starts with "Update:".
5. **Record:** `E/trends.md` (the 5 candidates, scores, choice, links); update the calendar row (topic, series, audience). The review email gets one line: "Why this topic today".
6. **Ideas inbox first:** if an unticked idea in this doc's Ideas inbox fits the slot and scores at least as well, use it and tick it.
7. **Sunday learning loop:** the Sunday 9:40 AM run pulls the last 7 days' reel insights (reach, views, skip rate, average watch time, likes, saves, shares) through Windsor.ai `get_data` for the Instagram account, writes `content/insights.md`, and notes which series and opening lines did best (lowest skip rate, longest average watch) against the targets under Reach levers, and compares the 7 AM and 8 PM slots (average reach and skip rate). When a slot is flexible, the best-performing series gets it next week. If insights are not available, it says so and carries on.
## Real-life scenarios, done safely
Real scenarios are what make a reel land. They stay within BCI Rule 36 when they are either dramatised (invented, built from a public pattern) or reported (public news or an official note) — never the owner's own clients or cases.
| Type | What it is | How it shows on screen | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dramatised scenario | Invented people and numbers, built from a pattern in reported cases or advisories | Tag "DRAMATISED · based on reported cases"; invented first name, age and city ("Ravi, 34, Pune"); masked numbers on mock screens | Must not resemble any real case or client; illustrative amounts are marked as such |
| Reported case | A case or police operation covered by mainstream news or an official press note | Source line "Reported: <outlet>, <date>"; "alleged" for anything not decided by a court | No names of victims or private accused; nothing that identifies a victim of a sexual offence; no prejudging a pending case |
| Official advisory | An I4C, CERT-In, RBI, NPCI, SEBI or police advisory | Source line "Source: I4C advisory, Aug 2026" | Paraphrase; a few words quoted at most |

Never: client stories, wins or testimonials; real phone numbers, IMEIs, UPI IDs or account numbers; agency logos, emblems or uniforms; photos of real people other than the owner's own cut-outs.
## Studio standard v2 (how the reels look from EP04)
Every reel from EP04 gets a series badge, a dramatised cold open on a realistic mock phone, a chapter label as each part starts, and a new visual beat every 2–3 seconds.
1. **Series identity:** badge at top-left with the series name and EP number, in the series colour; the same badge on the cover.
2. **Realistic mock phone screens** for the scenario: incoming call, WhatsApp chat, SMS, UPI payment or collect request, notification banners — masked data, DRAMATISED tag.
3. **Chapter cards** as a small top-corner label that changes with each part: 01 THE SCAM · 02 HOW IT WORKS · 03 THE TRAIL · 04 THE LAW · 05 DO THIS NOW, with a sound sting. No full-screen card: it costs seconds a 35–45 second reel doesn't have.
4. **Motion density:** 8–10 scenes; a new visual beat every 2–3 seconds; no static frame longer than 4 seconds.
5. **Money-flow network:** victim → layer 1 → layer 2 → layer 3 → cash-out (ATM, crypto, cheque, gold), with running timers and amount counters, and the frozen part marked HOLD.
6. **Keyword captions:** English keywords pop on screen in time with the Hindi voice, so the reel works with the sound off.
7. **Data cards:** every number on its own card with the source and date under it.
8. **Screenshot card:** the closing checklist (3 red flags, 3 actions) holds for 3 seconds before the outro, so it can be screenshotted.
9. **Sound:** the engine's original score, a sting on every chapter card, an impact on every big reveal.
10. **Cover:** series badge, a 2-line hook, the owner's cut-out photo and the topic's main visual; 3 options rendered.
11. **Quality check before review:** stills at every chapter card and every busy moment; no overlaps; body text at least 30 px; the review email lists which studio elements are in the reel.
**Engine work (once):** the first build run that uses this doc adds reusable components to the engine — `seriesBadge`, `chapterCard`, `callScreen`, `chatThread`, `smsCard`, `upiScreen`, `notificationBanner`, `moneyGraph`, `dataCard`, `kineticCaption`, `checklistCard` — checks them with stills, keeps every existing component working, and commits "Engine v3: studio components". Later runs reuse them.
## Reach levers that stay within the rules
Likes and shares come from a strong first 2 seconds, a scenario the viewer recognises, and a reason to send it on. None of that needs solicitation.
- **Hook in 1–2 seconds**, and the cover hook matches the reel's first line.
- **Open loop:** say early what they will know by the end ("the last step is the one that stops this").
- **Indian specifics:** UPI apps, ₹ amounts, a city, a festive sale, an e-challan, an electricity bill.
- **Hindi voice, English text:** reaches Hindi-first viewers and stays readable for corporate viewers.
- **Allowed share prompts:** "Save this", "Send it to your parents", "Forward it to your accounts team".
- **Not allowed:** "comment X / type X", "DM me", contact details, collaborations, paid promotion, "lawyer reveals".
- **Timing:** saveable explainers at 7 AM; story-led trending reels at 8 PM. Keep both slots for now: India's evening peak is around 7:30–9 PM, and the 8 PM reels averaged 90 accounts reached against 63 at 7 AM (three reels each, too few to decide). On Sunday 13 Dec 2026, if one slot is clearly weaker, the review email suggests a new time to the owner.
- **Series habit:** the same series on the same day each week (Corporate Cyber Brief on Wednesday and Friday nights).
- **Tags:** the owner's account plus rotating Approved pages from `content/tags.md` (unchanged).
- **Feedback loop:** the Sunday insights check (trend scan, step 7) shifts flexible slots towards what viewers actually save and share.
**Reach fixes, 25 Nov 2026.** The first 6 reels each reached 37–132 accounts, almost all non-followers, but 57–87% of viewers skipped in the first 3 seconds, average watch was 5–11 seconds, and the six got 1 share between them. For every reel built from 26 Nov 2026:
- **Length:** 35–45 seconds, one idea per reel.
- **Frame 1 is the hook:** the stake in big text and the first spoken line start together at 0 s. The logo is a small corner watermark, never an intro.
- **Send line:** in the last 5 seconds, in the voice and on screen, naming who needs it. The caption alone is not enough.
- **Plain words on screen:** at most one law card; section numbers live in the caption.
- **Targets per reel:** skip rate under 50%, average watch over 15 seconds. The Sunday loop reports each reel against them.
- **First hour:** the owner shares each new reel to his Story and to a few WhatsApp contacts or groups.
- **Make it about the viewer:** every hook and cover line speaks to "you" (your phone, your Aadhaar, your name, your family), and every reel ends with one check the viewer can do on their own phone in under a minute.
