# EP10 — Your files are locked. Your 6 hours start now.

**Series:** CORPORATE CYBER BRIEF (indigo badge) · **Primary audience:** CXOs, IT and security heads, and owners of SMEs, schools and
clinics that run their own server (second audience: accounts and admin teams who would see the ransom note first).
Send line: "Send this to your IT head."
Slot: 2026-11-27 pm (8:00 PM IST, Fri) · on-screen text English · voice **Hindi** (AI voice: the ElevenLabs voice set in the owner's
account) · "CERT-In" written as "Cert-In" and "IT head" as "आई-टी head" in the voice lines so they are spoken as words.
Format: **35–45 s** (strategy doc, "Reach fixes", 25 Nov 2026). Frame 1 is the hook at full opacity with the voice from 0.1 s (no
fade from black); small corner chapter labels with a sting; one law card + one number card; other sections in the caption; the
checklist holds for the screenshot; the send line is spoken and shown in the outro (the last 3 s).
**One idea:** when ransomware hits, the company must report it to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing it — and in the first hour,
isolate the systems and keep the logs, because they are the evidence.
Why this topic today: ransomware gangs keep listing new Indian victims (claims as late as 24 Nov), and last month a Maharashtra
hospital lost nine servers to an attack — yet many firms still don't know that CERT-In must hear about it within 6 hours, and that
ignoring CERT-In's directions can now cost up to ₹1 crore (see trends.md).
Scenario: **dramatised, based on reported cases** — the server name, file names, ransom note, 72-hour deadline and server logs
are invented, built from the pattern in reported Indian cases (files encrypted, a copy of the data taken, a crypto demand with a
deadline and a threat to publish). No real company, gang, person, IP address or account; no agency logo, emblem or seal.
Explain, never enable: entry routes appear only as two labels (phishing email, weak password); nothing on building, spreading
or paying for an attack, and nothing on hiding an incident.

Five-part spine: 01 THE ATTACK (scene 2) · 02 HOW IT WORKS (3) · 03 THE FIRST HOUR (4) · 04 THE LAW (5) · 05 DO THIS NOW (6–7).
Corporate Cyber Brief weighting: parts 1, 4 and 5 get the most time.
