# EP10 sources (pages opened 27 Nov 2026, 09:45–10:05 IST)

Every claim in the reel and caption, and the page(s) that support it. Pages marked (primary) are official or bare-Act text.

## CERT-In reporting rule (the reel's one idea)
- **PIB / MeitY press release, 18 May 2022** (primary) — https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1826388
  Supports: CERT-In's directions were issued "under sub-section (6) of section 70B of the Information Technology Act, 2000",
  dated 28.04.2022; FAQs released 18 May 2022.
- **Text of the Directions, 28 April 2022 (No. 20(3)/2022-CERT-In)** (reproduction) —
  https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/cert-in-issues-directions-relating-information-security-practices.html
  Supports: direction (ii) — incidents must be reported to CERT-In "within 6 hours of noticing such incidents or being brought to
  notice about such incidents"; applies to "service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporate and Government
  organisations"; reporting by email, phone (1800-11-4949) and fax (1800-11-6969), formats on cert-in.org.in; Annexure I item v —
  "Malicious code attacks such as spreading of virus/worm/Trojan/Bots/ Spyware/Ransomware/Cryptominers"; direction (iii) — a Point
  of Contact; direction (iv) — logs kept "for a rolling period of 180 days" within Indian jurisdiction and given to CERT-In on
  reporting; non-compliance "may invite punitive action under subsection (7) of the section 70B"; effective 60 days after issue.
- **LiveLaw column, 3 Jul 2022 (Koragal & Sharma)** — https://www.livelaw.in/columns/indian-computer-emergency-response-team-cert-in-information-technology-act-cert-in-rules-202834
  Supports: the six-hour rule and its reach (reputable secondary source).
- **CERT-In incident reporting form** (copy on a state IT department site) — https://cscoe.itewb.gov.in/upload/pages/certinirform.pdf
  Supports: reports go to CERT-In at incident@cert-in.org.in (fax +91-11-24368546). Used in the caption only as "cert-in.org.in";
  the email address is not shown in the reel.
- Not readable this run: https://www.cert-in.org.in/Directions70B.jsp (fetch refused) and the vikaspedia copy (no body text).

## Toll-free number (the one-minute phone check)
- **CERT-In Cyber Swachhta Kendra, "Prevention of Ransomware Infections"** (primary; CERT-In's own site) —
  https://www.csk.gov.in/alerts/ransomware.html — "Toll Free Phone: +91-1800-11-4949"; also supports the checklist line on paying:
  "Individuals or organizations are not encouraged to pay the ransom, as this does not guarantee files will be released. Report
  such instances of fraud to CERT-In and Law Enforcement agencies", and keeping backups offline.
- The same number appears in direction (ii) of the Directions (taxguru copy above).

## The penalty (the reel's one number card)
- **IT Act 2000, s.70B** (bare Act, original text) — https://lawx.in/acts/section/17/224/2244
  Supports: s.70B(6) power to call for information and give directions; s.70B(7) failure to comply "shall be punishable with
  imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year or with fine ... or with both".
- **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** (primary, official text) —
  https://www.csir.res.in/sites/default/files/2023-12/Jan-Vishwas-Act-2023.pdf
  Supports: Schedule entry 32 — "In section 70B, in sub-section (7), for the words "one lakh", the words "one crore" shall be
  substituted"; MeitY's office memorandum in the same file: appointed date 30 November 2023. So today: up to 1 year, or a fine up to
  ₹1 crore, or both. (Many explainers, including the LiveLaw column above, still say ₹1 lakh — outdated.)

## First-hour actions (scene 4 and the checklist)
- **CISA, "I've Been Hit By Ransomware!"** (US government, standards-level guidance) —
  https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ive-been-hit-ransomware — "Determine which systems were impacted, and immediately isolate
  them."; "Preserve evidence that is highly volatile in nature"; power down only if devices can't be disconnected.
- **CISA Ransomware Response Checklist (2022)** — https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/Ransomware_Response_Checklist_508.pdf
  — the isolation line; "Take a system image and memory capture of a sample of affected devices"; "CISA, MS-ISAC, and other
  federal law enforcement do not recommend paying ransom."
- CERT-In's own 180-day log rule (direction (iv), above) supports "keep the logs — they are evidence".
- "Don't wipe" = the plain-words version of CISA's "preserve evidence" plus CERT-In's log rule; the reel says "don't wipe
  anything", not "don't switch off".

## Law in the caption
- **BNS 2023 s.308 (extortion)** (bare Act) — https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/bharatiyanyayasanhita/308.php
  Supports: s.308(1) definition ("Whoever intentionally puts any person in fear of any injury ... and thereby dishonestly
  induces the person so put in fear to deliver to any person any property..."); s.308(2) up to seven years, or fine, or both.
  (devgan.in and latestlaws.com failed to load.)
- **DPDP Rules 2025, rule 7** — https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule7.html — intimate each affected Data Principal "without
  delay"; intimate the Board "without delay" and a detailed report "within seventy-two hours of becoming aware of the breach".
- **Commencement of rule 7**: Bar & Bench — https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025
  (notified 13 Nov 2025; rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 after 18 months, "i.e., May 13, 2027"); Khaitan & Co ERGO, 15 Nov 2025 —
  https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-11/ERGO%20-%20Digital%20Personal%20%20Data%20Protection%20Rules%20-%2015%20November%202025.pdf
  (core data-fiduciary obligations from 13 May 2027). So the caption says "from 13 May 2027", never "now".
- IT Act s.66 read with s.43 (computer-related offences, unauthorised access and damage) — cited in the caption as the usual
  sections for the attack itself; reported FIR practice: Medical Dialogues (below) reports BNS s.308 and "relevant provisions" of
  the IT Act.
