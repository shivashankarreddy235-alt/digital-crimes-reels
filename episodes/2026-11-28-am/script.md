# EP11 — Arrested? Your next 24 hours.

**Series:** KNOW YOUR RIGHTS (green badge) · **Primary audience:** everyday families (the people who get the call that
someone has been picked up) and young people; second audience: law students (saved as study notes).

Format: **35–45 s** (43.0 s with the voice). Frame 1 is the hook at full opacity; the voice starts at about 0.2 s. Small

**One idea:** the 24 hours after police hold you, in order — hour 0 → at the arrest → at the station → 2 hours before
court → the Magistrate — on a 24-hour clock.
Why this topic today: the owner set it on 27 Nov as the follow-up to EP10; the Supreme Court ruled this month (Sujit Das,
5 Nov 2026) that the clock starts the moment police hold you, and most families don't know what has to happen inside it.
Scenario: **dramatised** — an illustrated police-station desk at night, a wall clock that starts ticking and becomes the
24-hour dial (owner's request, 27 Nov). Plain silhouettes reused from EP10; no uniforms, badges, emblems, signboards, vehicle
markings or real people; DRAMATISED tag. Not EP10's street-and-van scene.
Not repeated: EP10's list of 11 rights (each right appears here only as a moment on the timeline) and anything about
cyber cases (EP12).

Five-part spine (35–45 s): 01 the scenario (hook, station desk) · 02 how it works (the 24-hour dial: hour 0, at the arrest,
at the station) · 03 the trail (the 2-hour written-grounds deadline on the dial) · 04 the law (the one law card + the
Magistrate's three options) · 05 do this now (free legal aid, emergency contact + 15100, screenshot card) · outro.

Accuracy notes: the times on the dial between hour 0 and the Magistrate are an example, and the reel says so; the 24 hours
exclude travel to the court (s.58); the written-grounds deadline is the outer limit ("at least 2 hours before remand") —
grounds are due in writing at the arrest unless that isn't possible (Mihir Rajesh Shah); bail at the station applies to
bailable offences only and needs a bail bond (s.478), so it is worded "can be given"; the Magistrate's options are shown with
"Nothing here is automatic"; no police-custody day count on screen (debated after BNSS s.187's wording change; the caption
names s.187 only); free legal aid for anyone in custody follows LSA Act s.12(g) (no income test in that clause).
