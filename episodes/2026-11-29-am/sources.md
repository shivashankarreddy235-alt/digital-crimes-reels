# EP13 sources — Never shared your OTP? You can still lose money (Myth vs Fact)

Every claim on screen, in the voiceover and in the caption, with the pages opened on 28 Nov 2026 (evening build).
Primary sources first. Quotes are short; the rest is paraphrase.

## Myth 1 — "To receive money, enter your PIN"
- **You never need a UPI PIN to receive money; scan a QR only to pay.** VERIFIED (primary).
  NPCI, UPI Safety Shield — https://npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi/upi-safety-shield : "Enter UPI PIN ONLY to deduct money from
  your account. UPI PIN is NOT required for receiving money"; "Scan QR ONLY for making payment and NOT for receiving money."
- **"A PIN on a 'Paying' screen sends money out"** (on screen). VERIFIED. PhonePe trust & safety blog, 4 Feb 2026 —
  https://www.phonepe.com/blog/trust-and-safety/jumped-deposit-scams-know-when-to-enter-your-upi-pin/ : look for "Paying" or
  "Sending to" at the top of the PIN screen; "you never need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money".
  Careful wording: NPCI's 13 Jan 2025 press release notes a PIN is also used for a balance enquiry
  (https://npci.org.in/PDF/npci/press-releases/2025/NPCI-Press-Release-Clarification-on-the-Jumped-Deposit-Scam-dated-13-January-2025.pdf),
  so the reel does not say "a PIN only ever pays": the voice and the screenshot card say a PIN is never needed to receive;
  the screen ties "money out" to the "Paying" screen.
- Scenario (dramatised): a "cashback" chat with a QR "to receive" money. Pattern reported by PhonePe's festive warning
  (Telangana Today, 29 Oct 2026 — https://telanganatoday.com/phonepe-warns-users-against-diwali-qr-code-scams-shares-safety-tips :
  "users do not need to enter their UPI PIN or make a payment to receive money"). The QR on screen is a random pattern,
  not a real code; no app or bank brand.
- Why not a "collect request" scene: NPCI stopped person-to-person UPI collect requests from 1 Oct 2025 (circular of
  29 Jul 2025; merchants can still use them) — Business Today, 13 Aug 2025:
  https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/banking/story/npci-to-tighten-upi-rules-peer-to-peer-collect-feature-to-end-in-october-489244-2025-08-13

## Myth 3 — "The bank never pays it back" (the one law card)
- **Today's rule** — RBI circular RBI/2017-18/15, 6 Jul 2017, "Customer Protection – Limiting Liability of Customers in
  Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions" — https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11040 . VERIFIED (primary):
  - para 6(i): zero liability for "Contributory fraud/ negligence/ deficiency on the part of the bank (irrespective of
    whether or not the transaction is reported by the customer)";
  - para 6(ii): third-party breach, deficiency neither with the bank nor the customer: zero liability if the customer
    notifies the bank "within three working days of receiving the communication from the bank";
  - para 7(i): customer negligence (e.g. sharing payment credentials): the customer bears the entire loss until reporting;
    para 7(ii): 4–7 working days, liability capped (Table 1);
  - para 9: credit (shadow reversal) within 10 working days; para 10: resolve within 90 days;
  - para 12: "The burden of proving customer liability ... shall lie on the bank."
  - para 5: banks must ask customers to register for SMS alerts; banks may not offer electronic transactions (other than
    ATM cash withdrawals) to customers who give no mobile number.
  Law card wording: "Fraud not your fault (a bank lapse, or a breach elsewhere)? Report it within 3 working days of the
  bank's alert, and your liability is zero. The bank must prove any fault was yours." (paras 6, 12).
- **Courts apply it** (background, not on screen): Kerala HC, SBI ordered to refund ₹3.73 lakh where the bank could not
  prove negligence and the customer reported the same day (LiveLaw, 19 Oct 2026,
  https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/kerala-high-court/kerala-high-court-cybercrime-zero-liability-sbi-555268 ); Gauhati HC
  (Moneylife, 3 Dec 2025, https://moneylife.in/article/gauhati-hc-upholds-zero-liability-in-cyber-fraud-orders-sbi-to-refund-94204-to-customer/79018.html ).
  The other side: Delhi HC (DB), SBI v. Hare Ram Singh, 2026:DHC:4833-DB — negligence is not limited to sharing an OTP
  and needs evidence (Bar & Bench, 30 May 2026,
  https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/customer-clicking-suspicious-links-despite-warnings-cant-blame-bank-for-losing-money-to-cyberfraud-delhi-hc ;
  Verdictum, 1 Jun 2026, https://www.verdictum.in/delhi-high-court/state-bank-of-india-v-hare-ram-singh-anr-2026dhc4833-db-cuber-fraud-1615109 ).
  So the reel never says "the bank always pays": "not your fault + reported fast".

## From 1 Jan 2027 (the one number card)
- RBI press release 2026-2027/533, 24 Jun 2026, "RBI Issues Amendment Directions on 'Review of Framework of Limiting
  Customer Liability in Digital Transactions'" — https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63011 :
  the directions "shall come into effect from January 1, 2027" (draft issued 6 Mar 2026). VERIFIED (primary).
- Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Third Amendment Directions, 2026,
  RBI/2026-27/167, 24 Jun 2026 — https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13543 . VERIFIED (primary):
  - para 3(2): applies to electronic banking transactions on or after 1 Jan 2027; replaces section D (paras 64–76) of the
    2025 Directions with "DA. Customer Protection in Fraudulent Electronic Banking Transactions";
  - para 76K: burden of proving customer liability on the bank; para 76L: zero liability for the bank's negligence or
    deficiency, reported or not;
  - para 76M: third-party breach — zero liability if reported "within five calendar days from the date of its
    occurrence" (on screen: "From 1 Jan 2027: within 5 calendar days");
  - para 76N: customer negligence — liable until reporting, to the extent not eligible for compensation; para 4(20C) lists
    negligence examples, including giving credentials to another person and not updating contact details;
  - para 76T: small-value compensation — gross loss up to ₹50,000, individuals (incl. sole proprietors), negligence cases
    under 76N, bona fide loss: "85 per cent of the net loss amount ... or ₹25,000, whichever is less, once during her /
    his lifetime"; reported on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or helpline 1930 and to the bank within five
    calendar days; paid by the bank within five calendar days of the application (RBI reimburses most of it); para 76U:
    covers losses up to one year from the effective date.
  Reel wording: "Even if you were tricked: up to ₹25,000 back on a small loss: 85% of a loss up to ₹50,000 · once in a
  lifetime · for individuals, a genuine loss · report within 5 days to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in and to your bank ·
  in force from 1 Jan 2027". Voice: "from 1 January, even if the mistake was yours, up to 25,000 back on a small loss".
- Secondary, consistent: Business Today, 26 Jun 2026 —
  https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/rbis-new-digital-fraud-compensation-rules-from-january-2027-how-victims-can-recover-up-to-rs25000-539435-2026-06-26 ;
  ELP note (Jun 2026) — https://elplaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RBI-Tightens-Customer-Protection-Framework-for-Fraudulent-Electronic-Banking-Transactions.pdf ;
  Taxmann — https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-revises-customer-liability-framework-for-digital-transactions/ .
  (Moneylife's report could not be opened.) The new rules apply to commercial banks by this notification; other bank
  categories got their own parallel amendment directions on the same day (press release) — the reel says "RBI's rules",
  not "every bank".

## The one-minute check — your registered mobile number
- Why: today's zero-liability clock runs from "receiving the communication from the bank" (2017 circular, para 6(ii)); banks
  must send SMS alerts to customers who register (para 5); from 1 Jan 2027 not updating contact details counts as
  negligence (2026 Directions, para 4(20C)). The check: open your bank app, confirm the registered mobile is the number you
  use today. Mock screen: generic "Your bank app", masked number +91 98XXX XXX12, no bank name or logo.
